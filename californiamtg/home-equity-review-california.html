Need Equity? Do Not Automatically Replace the First Mortgage You Already Have.

If your current mortgage has a rate you want to preserve, start by comparing a HELOC or fixed home-equity loan against a cash-out refinance. The right answer depends on your current first mortgage, the amount you need and the total monthly and long-term cost.

The decision

Borrow only the new money — or replace the whole first mortgage?

That is the question many California homeowners should answer before starting a cash-out refinance. A second-lien structure can leave the existing first mortgage in place, while a cash-out refinance creates a new first mortgage for the entire balance plus the cash being withdrawn.

Preserve the first mortgage

A HELOC or home-equity loan may let an eligible homeowner access equity without repricing the existing first lien.

Choose flexible or fixed

Depending on the available program, a revolving HELOC and a fixed lump-sum home-equity loan solve different cash-flow needs.

Compare the full math

The existing rate, first-mortgage balance, new cash need, fees, payment structure and expected holding period all matter.

Why this comparison matters more in a high-rate environment

Many homeowners still carry first mortgages originated when rates were materially lower. Replacing that entire balance just to access a smaller amount of new cash can change the economics of a cash-out refinance. A second-lien review separates the old money from the new money and lets the homeowner compare both structures before making that trade.

HELOC, fixed home-equity loan or cash-out refinance?

  • HELOC: revolving access can fit staged projects or liquidity needs when an eligible lender offers the desired line and terms.
  • Fixed home-equity loan: a lump sum with a fixed rate and term can fit a known one-time cash need.
  • Cash-out refinance: replaces the first mortgage and may still be preferable when the full-loan economics work better for the borrower.

Good referral conversations

  • A homeowner wants to remodel or build an ADU but has a low-rate first mortgage.
  • A client needs a down payment for another property before selling or refinancing the current home.
  • A homeowner is considering debt consolidation and wants to compare secured borrowing costs.
  • A borrower asked for cash-out but may be repricing a much larger first-mortgage balance than necessary.
