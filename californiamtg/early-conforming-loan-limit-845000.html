Early Conforming Loan Limit Up to $845,000

Select lenders are offering an early conforming limit up to $845,000 before FHFA publishes its official 2027 limits. California borrowers should compare that figure with the property county's current official limit before assuming a loan is jumbo.

The numbers at a glance

Reference1-unit amountWhat it means
Official 2026 national baseline$832,750Current FHFA baseline for most U.S. counties
Early lender conforming limitUp to $845,000Available through select lenders; guidelines vary
Difference$12,250Additional potential conforming room where the baseline applies
Official 2026 high-cost ceilingUp to $1,249,125County-specific; many California high-cost counties have limits above the baseline
Official 2027 FHFA limitPendingNot yet announced as of Sept. 12, 2026
Do not replace the county table with $845,000. The $845,000 figure is not an FHFA county limit. California MTG's county-line tools should continue to use official 2026 FHFA limits while showing the early lender amount as a separate financing option.

When the early $845,000 limit can matter

If the property is in a county where the official 2026 one-unit conforming limit is $832,750, a proposed loan such as $840,000 sits above the official baseline. A select lender's early $845,000 program may create another conforming path, subject to that lender's rules.

When it may not change anything

Many California high-cost counties already have official 2026 conforming limits above $845,000. In those counties, a loan around $840,000 may already fit within the existing county limit. The correct comparison is always property county + unit count + proposed loan amount.
