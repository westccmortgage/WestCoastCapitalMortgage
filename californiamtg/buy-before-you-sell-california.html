California Move-Up Buyer Review

Buy the Next Home Before You Sell the Current One.

If your down payment or buying power is tied up in a California home you already own, review the equity and timing before writing a sale-contingent offer. The right structure may let an eligible homeowner purchase first and sell on a separate timeline.

The problem

Your equity is real. The timing is the obstacle.

Move-up buyers often have substantial equity but cannot use the sale proceeds until the existing home closes. That can create a weak home-sale contingency, a rushed sale, or a missed property. The financing review should happen before the offer, not after the contingency becomes a problem.

Buy before selling

Review whether equity in the departing residence can support a bridge or other eligible structure for the next purchase.

Make a cleaner offer

For an eligible borrower, the goal may be to remove the home-sale dependency from the next purchase. Financing is not the same as a cash offer.

Protect the sale timeline

A buyer who is not forced to close both homes on the same day may have more flexibility to prepare, list and negotiate the departing residence.

Which financing paths may be worth comparing?

The answer depends on the borrower and both properties. Possible structures may include a consumer bridge loan, an eligible home-equity structure, a portfolio solution, or conventional/jumbo financing paired with a planned payoff or recast after the departing residence sells. Not every structure works when the current home is already listed, and not every borrower can carry or collateralize both properties.

Who should review this early?

  • Move-up buyers whose down payment is locked in the current home.
  • Downsizers who have substantial equity but do not want to sell first and move twice.
  • Buyers who keep losing to offers without a home-sale contingency.
  • Homeowners relocating within California or between states.
  • Agents with a seller who also needs to become the next buyer.

Why this is also a Realtor conversation

A listing client who says “we cannot buy until this home sells” may have a financing problem before they have a real-estate problem. Reviewing the equity before the listing goes live can clarify whether the client has another path. Buyer agents can also identify the issue before a desired property appears and the offer clock starts.
