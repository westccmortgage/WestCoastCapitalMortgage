Non-Warrantable Condo Financing: When the Building — Not the Borrower — Is the Problem

A condo mortgage can fail even after the borrower looks fully qualified. The lender is underwriting two things: the borrower and the condo project.

Reviewed August 20, 2026 · Anatoliy Kanevsky · NMLS #2775380 · CA Broker DRE #01385024

Quick answer

If a California condo loan is declined because of the project, first identify the exact project-level issue. Fannie Mae's current project standards treat project eligibility risk separately from the borrower's individual credit risk. Depending on the issue, an alternative lender or portfolio / Non-QM path may be worth reviewing, but the solution must match the actual problem.

Why a condo can be harder than a house

With a detached home, the lender mainly evaluates the borrower and the individual property. With a condo, the lender also evaluates the project: its financial stability, physical condition, insurance, legal issues, ownership structure and other project characteristics.

Fannie Mae's current Selling Guide specifically identifies project-level issues such as litigation or pre-litigation activity, single-entity ownership, projects in need of critical repairs, certain commercial-space situations and other ineligible characteristics.

Common project issues that can stop a conventional condo loan

  • Material litigation or pre-litigation involving the HOA or project.
  • Critical repairs, structural concerns or significant deferred maintenance.
  • Insurance coverage that does not meet the selected program's requirements.
  • Project financial or reserve issues.
  • Excessive commercial or non-residential space under the applicable review.
  • Concentrated ownership by a single individual or entity.
  • Project structure or use that falls outside agency eligibility.

Do not diagnose the project from a one-word denial

“Non-warrantable” is often used as a catch-all description, but the financing strategy depends on the exact reason. A litigation issue is not the same as an insurance issue. A reserve problem is not the same as a single-entity ownership problem. A lender who simply says “project not eligible” has not yet given you enough information to choose the next loan.

What to request immediately

  • The lender's specific project denial reason.
  • Completed condo / HOA questionnaire if one was obtained.
  • Current master insurance information.
  • HOA budget and financial statements if available.
  • Reserve study or structural / repair documentation if relevant.
  • Litigation or legal disclosures if that was the issue.
  • Special-assessment information.
  • Purchase contract and closing deadline if the buyer is already in escrow.

Why 2026 reserve and project review rules deserve attention

Agency condo guidance continues to evolve. Freddie Mac's current condo guidance, for example, includes updated reserve-study methodology requirements for certain mortgages with application dates on or after August 3, 2026. That is one more reason to use current project documents rather than rely on how the same building was financed several years ago.

Possible financing paths after a project denial

The correct path depends on occupancy, loan amount, credit, down payment, project problem and lender appetite. Depending on the scenario, a broker may compare:

  • Another conventional lender if the first denial involved an overlay or incomplete project review.
  • A portfolio lender with different condo-project guidelines.
  • A Non-QM condo program for an eligible owner-occupied, second-home or investment scenario.
  • A DSCR path for an eligible business-purpose investment property.
  • A different leverage structure when additional equity changes available options.

A useful pre-escrow strategy

When a building has known litigation, insurance, reserve, repair or ownership-concentration concerns, reviewing the project before the borrower spends weeks in underwriting can save time. The earlier the project issue is identified, the more realistic the financing plan and closing timeline can be.
