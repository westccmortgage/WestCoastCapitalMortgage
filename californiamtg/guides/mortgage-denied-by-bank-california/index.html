Mortgage Denied by a Bank in California? Diagnose the Denial Before You Reapply

A bank's “no” can be final for that program without being final for the borrower. The first job is to understand exactly what failed — income, credit, property, reserves, appraisal, loan size, or a lender-specific overlay.

Reviewed August 20, 2026 · Anatoliy Kanevsky · NMLS #2775380 · CA Broker DRE #01385024

Quick answer

Do not immediately submit the same file to five more lenders. Get the denial reason, separate borrower issues from property issues, and determine whether the problem is a universal guideline or simply that lender's program. Then choose the next loan path.

Step 1: identify the exact reason

“The bank declined me” is not enough information. The useful question is: what specific underwriting rule caused the decline?

  • Qualifying income was too low.
  • Debt-to-income ratio was too high.
  • Credit score, credit history or a recent credit event did not fit.
  • Insufficient down payment or reserves.
  • The loan amount exceeded that lender's program.
  • The property or appraisal did not meet guidelines.
  • A condo project was not eligible under the selected program.
  • Self-employed income was calculated differently than expected.
  • The lender applied an internal overlay beyond the basic program rules.

If the problem is self-employed income

A traditional bank may rely heavily on filed tax-return income. A broker can compare whether the borrower still qualifies conventionally or whether bank statements, 1099 income, a P&L, assets or another alternative-documentation path should be reviewed.

If the problem is the property, changing income documents will not solve it

Some denials are property problems: condo project eligibility, appraisal concerns, property condition, unusual use, non-warrantable characteristics or another program restriction. In that case the correct next step is a lender whose property guidelines fit — not simply a new income calculation.

If the loan crossed the conforming or high-balance line

In California, county loan limits matter. A loan can move from conforming to high-balance to jumbo depending on the property location and loan amount. A small change in down payment or structure can sometimes change the review path completely.

If the property is an investment

A borrower denied under personal-income underwriting may have a separate business-purpose DSCR path if the subject property is an investment and the rental economics support it. DSCR is not for an owner-occupied home.

Read the California DSCR guide →

If credit is the issue

Do not assume a Non-QM label automatically solves weak credit. Alternative programs still have credit standards, and pricing and leverage can change materially based on credit history. The review should identify whether waiting, reducing the loan amount, adding reserves, or using another eligible structure changes the result.

If reserves or cash-to-close are the issue

Sometimes the borrower technically qualifies but the selected program requires more post-closing reserves or cash than expected. The comparison should include not only the down payment, but also closing costs, prepaid items and reserve requirements.

What to bring for a useful second look

  • The denial or explanation from the original lender, if available.
  • Purchase contract or property address if already under contract.
  • Estimated loan amount and down payment.
  • Income type and key documentation already provided.
  • Approximate credit range.
  • Assets and reserves.
  • Any appraisal or condo/project issue raised by the first lender.
  • Closing deadline, if there is one.
