1099 Mortgage California: Compare the Income Before Choosing the Program

Being paid on a 1099 does not automatically mean you cannot get a traditional mortgage — and it does not automatically mean a 1099-specific program is best. The useful comparison is which income method gives the strongest eligible file.

Reviewed August 20, 2026 · Anatoliy Kanevsky · NMLS #2775380 · CA Broker DRE #01385024

Quick answer

California independent contractors, consultants and other 1099 earners may have several mortgage paths. Depending on the scenario, a lender may qualify income from filed tax returns, an alternative 1099 calculation, bank deposits, a current P&L, assets or another approved Non-QM method. The same borrower can qualify very differently under each method.

Why 1099 income creates confusion

A 1099 shows gross payments received from a payer; it does not by itself show the borrower's true business expenses or final qualifying income. Traditional underwriting may rely on tax returns and business-income analysis, while an alternative 1099 program may use a different lender-specific approach.

Who commonly needs this comparison

  • Independent contractors paid by one or several companies.
  • Consultants, sales professionals and commission-oriented contractors.
  • Medical, technology, creative and professional-service workers paid on 1099s.
  • Borrowers who recently moved from W-2 employment to contractor status.
  • Business owners whose 1099s represent only one part of a larger income picture.

Four qualifying paths to compare

1. Traditional tax-return income

If tax returns support the mortgage, conventional, high-balance or jumbo financing may still be the most efficient option. Do not dismiss it just because the borrower receives 1099s.

2. Alternative 1099 income

Some Non-QM lenders offer programs specifically designed around a borrower's 1099 history. The qualifying formula, required history and expense treatment are program-specific, so a lender comparison matters.

3. Bank statement income

If deposits show the business cash flow more clearly than the 1099s alone, a bank-statement program may produce a stronger result.

4. P&L or other alternative documentation

For some borrowers, a lender-approved profit-and-loss or other alternative-documentation path may better represent current earnings. Availability and verification requirements vary.

What matters more than the 1099 label

  • How long the income source has existed.
  • Whether income is stable, increasing or declining.
  • How many payers or clients generate the income.
  • Whether the borrower has meaningful business expenses.
  • How the 1099 income appears on filed tax returns.
  • Cash reserves, down payment, credit profile and property type.
  • Whether the borrower has other W-2, rental, investment or business income.

Recent switch from W-2 to 1099

A recent change in employment structure can be one of the more difficult scenarios because lenders care about continuity and history, not just the current annualized number. The correct review looks at the borrower's profession, prior work history, current contract structure and which lenders have guidelines that fit the transition.

What to prepare for the first review

  • Most recent 1099s and approximate year-to-date income.
  • Time in the same profession or line of work.
  • Business entity, if any.
  • Recent bank-deposit pattern.
  • Tax-return income, if available.
  • Property location, price/value and target loan amount.
  • Down payment/equity, credit range and reserves.
