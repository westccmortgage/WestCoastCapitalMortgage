2027 Conforming Loan Limit

On November 24, 2026, FHFA announced that the 2027 baseline conforming loan limit for one-unit homes rises to $855,400, up $22,650 from $832,750. In high-cost areas the ceiling is $1,283,100.

2026 vs. 2027 conforming loan limits

One-unit homes. County limits can be anywhere between the baseline and the ceiling.

Limit1-unit amountNote
2026 baseline$832,750Applies through 2026
2027 baseline$855,400Up $22,650 from 2026; applies in most U.S. counties
2027 high-cost ceiling$1,283,100150% of the baseline; the highest limit in the contiguous U.S.
Early lender limits (September 2026)$845,000–$850,000Offered by some lenders before FHFA’s announcement; the official 2027 limit is higher

What changes for borrowers

  • In a baseline county, a first mortgage between $832,750 and $855,400 may now fit under the conforming limit instead of being a jumbo loan.
  • In a high-cost county, the 2027 county limit can be well above $855,400, up to $1,283,100, so a larger loan may still be conforming.
  • The new limits apply to loans that Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac acquire in 2027. Lenders differ on timing, so ask which limit your lender uses for your closing date.

Example: $850,000 first mortgage

In a baseline county, $850,000 is above the 2026 limit of $832,750 but below the 2027 limit of $855,400, so it may be conforming rather than jumbo once the 2027 limit applies. Unit count, occupancy and lender guidelines still matter.
