2026 California Jumbo Loan Limits by County

Before calling a loan “jumbo,” check the property county and unit count. California limits vary materially by county.

Quick answer: the 2026 baseline conforming loan limit for a one-unit property is $832,750. In high-cost areas, the one-unit ceiling can reach $1,249,125. The correct limit is based on the property location, not the borrower’s home address.

When does a California loan become jumbo?

A loan is generally considered jumbo when the loan amount exceeds the applicable FHFA conforming loan limit for the property county and number of units. That is why a $1.1 million loan can fall into a different category in Orange County than in Riverside County.

Why down payment can change the path

The purchase price itself does not determine whether a mortgage is jumbo. The loan amount does. A buyer near the county line may be able to compare a jumbo structure with an alternative down-payment structure that brings the first mortgage at or below the applicable conforming limit. Whether that is financially better depends on pricing, liquidity, reserves, taxes, and the borrower’s complete scenario.

Examples

Orange County purchase

A proposed one-unit first mortgage of $1,200,000 is below Orange County’s 2026 one-unit conforming ceiling of $1,249,125. A proposed $1,300,000 first mortgage is above that county limit and would generally move into jumbo review.

Riverside County purchase

A proposed one-unit first mortgage of $900,000 exceeds Riverside County’s 2026 one-unit conforming limit of $832,750, so the same loan amount can be treated differently than it would be in Los Angeles or Orange County.

Official source

Source: Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA), 2026 Conforming Loan Limit Values. FHFA announced the 2026 one-unit national baseline of $832,750 and the high-cost ceiling of $1,249,125. County limits can change annually, so verify the current year before relying on an estimate.
