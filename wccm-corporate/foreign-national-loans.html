Foreign National Loans for International Buyers

Buying a home or investment property in California or Florida from abroad? International buyers can often finance US real estate through foreign national loan programs — frequently without a US credit score or Social Security Number.

Can international buyers finance property in the US?

Yes. Every year, many international buyers purchase second homes and investment property in the United States — and California and Florida are two of the most popular destinations. You do not always need to pay all cash. Foreign national loan programs are designed for buyers who are not US citizens and may not have US credit, a Social Security Number, or US-based income.

These programs use alternative documentation — such as your passport, income and bank references from your home country, and reserves — so qualified international buyers can finance a purchase instead of tying up all of their capital.

FAQ

Foreign national loans — common questions

Can a foreign national get a mortgage in the United States?

Yes. Foreign national loan programs are designed for non-US citizens and non-residents who want to buy property in the US. Eligibility, terms, and documentation vary by lender and program and are subject to underwriting review.

Do I need a US credit score or Social Security Number?

Often no. Many foreign national programs use alternative documentation such as a passport, foreign credit references, and international bank statements instead of a US credit score or SSN. Requirements vary by lender.

How much down payment do foreign national buyers usually need?

Foreign national loans typically require a larger down payment than standard mortgages. The exact amount depends on the lender, property type, and the borrower’s profile, and is subject to program guidelines.

Can I buy an investment property as a foreign national?

Yes. Many international buyers purchase second homes or investment property. Depending on the scenario, foreign national or DSCR programs (which can review the property’s rental income) may fit.

What documents are usually needed?

Commonly a valid passport (and visa if applicable), proof of income from your home country, international bank statements and reserves, and sometimes a reference letter from your foreign bank. Requirements vary by lender.
