2027 Conforming Loan Limit

On November 24, 2026, FHFA announced that the 2027 baseline conforming loan limit for one-unit homes rises to $855,400, up $22,650 from $832,750. In high-cost areas the ceiling is $1,283,100.

What changed

2026 vs. 2027 conforming loan limits

The limits follow home prices: FHFA’s house price index rose 2.72% between the third quarters of 2025 and 2026, and the baseline rises by the same percentage.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 2027 conforming loan limit?
For one-unit homes in most of the U.S., the 2027 baseline conforming loan limit is $855,400, up $22,650 from $832,750 in 2026. FHFA announced it on November 24, 2026.
What is the 2027 limit in high-cost areas?
The 2027 ceiling for one-unit homes in high-cost areas is $1,283,100, which is 150% of the baseline. Each county's actual limit depends on its median home value, so many California counties fall between the baseline and the ceiling.
When do the 2027 limits apply?
The new limits apply to loans Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac can acquire in 2027. Some lenders may accept the higher limit for loans closing earlier; ask us which lender options fit your timeline.
Is an $850,000 loan jumbo in California?
Not under the 2027 limits: $850,000 is below the $855,400 baseline. In 2026 the same loan was above the $832,750 baseline in baseline counties. The property's county, unit count and lender guidelines decide the final classification.
