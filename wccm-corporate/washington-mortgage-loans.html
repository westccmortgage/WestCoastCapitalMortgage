Seller credits & buydowns

Can a Seller Pay Closing Costs in Washington?

Direct answer: Yes, in eligible transactions. Seller credits are governed by the mortgage program and interested-party contribution limits, not by a blanket statewide rule. They may cover eligible closing costs and, in some structures, a rate buydown.

Can Seller Credits Be Used for a Mortgage Rate Buydown?

Direct answer: Sometimes. A seller-funded temporary or permanent buydown must fit the applicable program rules and contribution limits. Under Fannie Mae guidance, temporary buydown eligibility is limited by transaction type and the borrower is qualified using the note rate, not the reduced initial payment rate.

Temporary buydown

Can lower the borrower’s initial payment for an eligible period while leaving the mortgage note terms unchanged.

Permanent buydown

Uses discount points or another allowable structure to reduce the note rate for the life of the loan, subject to pricing and program rules.

Are Washington buyers getting more negotiating opportunities?

Opportunities can vary by city, neighborhood, property type, days on market and seller motivation. Rather than labeling the entire state a buyer’s market, evaluate each listing for price flexibility, closing-cost credits, buydown contributions and other terms that improve the financing structure.

Washington Mortgage Questions

Can a seller pay closing costs in Washington?
Yes, seller-paid closing costs may be permitted subject to the loan program, occupancy, loan-to-value, sales price, appraisal and applicable interested-party contribution limits. Seller credits cannot automatically be used for every borrower expense or required borrower contribution.
Can seller credits be used for a mortgage rate buydown?
They can be used in some eligible transactions for temporary or permanent rate buydowns, subject to program rules and interested-party contribution limits. A temporary buydown does not change the note rate used to qualify the borrower.
What mortgage options are available for self-employed Washington buyers?
Potential paths include conventional underwriting using tax returns, bank-statement programs, other Non-QM structures, and in some cases asset-based or asset-utilization qualification. The right path depends on the borrower, property and documentation.
Can investors use DSCR financing in Washington?
Yes, eligible investment-property scenarios may use DSCR financing, which generally evaluates property cash flow under lender-specific rules rather than relying primarily on employment income.
When should a Washington borrower compare jumbo and conventional financing?
When the loan amount approaches or exceeds conforming or high-cost limits, or when borrower assets, reserves, property type or pricing make a jumbo execution competitive. The comparison should be made before an offer or rate decision whenever possible.
